The CVPR review form (pre-rebuttal review), after the official CVPR 2026 reviewer training and
guidelines and the CVPR 2024 program chairs' form outline. Write `review.md` with these headings,
in this order:

## Summary
2-4 sentences in your own words: what the paper does, what it claims, and why it matters if true.

## Strengths
What gives the paper value, with specific evidence: experimentally validated ideas, an insightful
organization of related work, new tools, impressive results. Above all, what can someone
interested in the topic learn from the paper?

## Weaknesses
What detracts from the contributions, with evidence and locations: missing controlled experiments,
misleading claims, technical errors, whether the method can be understood and reproduced.
Official guidance: "Bullet lists with one short phrase per bullet are NOT a detailed review."

## Preliminary recommendation
One level of the rating scale given with the output files, as `<number> - <label>`.

## Justification
Why the paper should be accepted or not: which strengths and weaknesses weigh most and how they
lead to the rating, constructive suggestions, and the questions you want answered in the
rebuttal. Ask only for clarifications or small experiments that fit a one-week rebuttal with
academic resources.

## Additional comments
Minor suggestions, typos, corrections; not crucial to the recommendation.

## Confidence
One level of the confidence scale, as `<number> - <label>`, plus one sentence on your expertise
for this paper.

Official reviewer rules (CVPR 2026 guidelines; {venue} call for papers):
- Not beating the state of the art on a benchmark is not by itself grounds for rejection. Weigh
  novelty and potential impact together with performance.
- A claim that the work "has been done before" must name specific references and say how close
  they are. "This is well known" is not enough.
- Minor flaws that are easy to correct are no reason to reject.
- Do not ask for substantial new experiments (what a major revision would need), and do not
  penalize their absence.
- Prior work for {venue}: papers that appeared online after {cutoff} are contemporaneous; the
  submission is not rejected because of comparison to them. arXiv and published papers count
  alike. Date alone does not make a comparison mandatory.
- An honest limitations discussion counts for the paper, not against it.
- Refer to "the paper", not "you" or "the authors". No belittling or sarcastic comments.
- Requests for comparisons with methods that have no public code must be justified.
- Evidence bars (CVPR 2024 PCs): a potentially transformative idea needs a basic proof of concept;
  an established problem with a plausible idea needs benchmark results; a weird, overly complex or
  seemingly incremental method needs extraordinary results, scrutinized carefully.
- Acceptance criteria (CVPR 2026 AC guide). Oral: potential to be very significant. Poster:
  incremental steps that expand the community's knowledge, useful tools, interest to a
  subcommunity, or creative ideas that are hard to judge but plausible. Reject: unlikely to be
  significant or already known, with clear evidence.

Real reviewers do not all follow these rules. Your persona card says how faithfully you follow them.
